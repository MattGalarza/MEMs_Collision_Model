# Julia verification of "Squeeze films between doubly tapered plates closing to contact"

    julia verify_squeeze_films.jl        # installs Plots, LaTeXStrings, QuadGK, SpecialFunctions on first run

Set `QUICK = true` at the top for a ~2 minute pass on coarser grids; the full run takes roughly 5–15 minutes. The script prints a PASS/FAIL line per check, writes six PDF figures to `figures/`, and exits with code 1 if anything fails. `reference_tables.jl` holds the paper's Appendix E tables, generated programmatically from the solver output (`maps.json`), not retyped.

**Part 1 (derivations, 256-bit arithmetic).** Every identity of §2–§6 is evaluated at random parameters, with derivatives by central differences and integrals by adaptive Gauss–Kronrod. Covered: the velocity profile and its slip conditions; the flux law and dissipation; Proposition 2.1, checked directly by integrating continuity across a gap whose walls, wall speeds, pressure and slip length all vary in x and t; the Womersley limit; the corner ODE; Proposition 5.1's corner pressures, checked against the PDE and all three edge conditions; the slip-corner constant; the strip law; the small-taper series; the closed form (4.3); $J_B$, $J_C$; the slice function $F$, its limits and its relation to $\Psi$; the sum rules; $c_0$ from the Glaisher–Kinkelin constant; the constants $C_X$ as printed; the geometric means; every scaling coefficient; homogeneity. A correct identity leaves a residual near $10^{-60}$, a wrong one $O(1)$; Figure 1 plots them.

**Part 2 (cases).** The finite-volume solver is a line-by-line port of the one that produced the paper's tables, on the same grids, so the maps should reproduce to about $10^{-4}$. The P1 Galerkin solver integrates the cubic mobility exactly, so its damping is a rigorous lower bound (pressure principle). Checks: the screened logarithm (sealed and open tip) bracketed FE ≤ exact ≤ FV; nine map entries; class-A slip saturation; exact scaling; monotonicity in taper, slip and venting; tip-venting ratios; corner amplitudes, exponents and the slip-corner logarithm; the strip law for one and two vents; the small-taper constant; FE ≤ FV on a corner class.

**What was and was not executed before delivery.** Julia binaries could not be installed where this was written, so the script has not been run. It parses cleanly under the tree-sitter Julia grammar, and the two checks with a nontrivial expected outcome were calibrated with Python replicas on identical grids:

* *Screened logarithm.* The bracket holds at $h_{\min} = 10^{-9}, 10^{-10}, 10^{-11}$ (e.g. FE 92.96 ≤ 93.65 ≤ FV 93.84 at $10^{-10}$) and for the open tip. It fails at $10^{-8}$ ($\Lambda = 0.013$), where the asymptote itself is about 1.5% low, so that point is plotted but not asserted.
* *Small-taper constant.* Extrapolating $(\Phi_B - 12\ln(\alpha/\beta))/12$ to $\beta/\alpha \to 0$ from $\beta/\alpha = 0.001, 0.002, 0.005$ gives −0.5810 (FE lower bound), −0.5551 (FV) and −0.5593 (refined FV), against the prediction −0.5609. The prediction is bracketed, and refinement closes the gap to 0.0016. The coarse-grid figure −0.5551 alone would wrongly suggest a 0.006 discrepancy.

If a check fails on your machine, the PASS/FAIL line prints both numbers; the most likely culprits are plotting-backend details (fonts, LaTeX labels), which do not affect the numerics.
